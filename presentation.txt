🏆 Complete 4-Person Presentation Guide (~3.5 Minutes)
👥 Team Setup:
Person D (Frontend Lead) holds the laptop / controls the mouse.
Person A (Data Engineer), Person B (AI Engineer), Person C (Backend/Safety Engineer), and Person D stand together facing the judges.
⏱️ PHASE 1: Hook & Problem Statement (0:00 – 0:45)
Speaker: Person D (Frontend Lead)
Screen Action: Open on Tab 1: Live Overview.

Person D: *"Hello judges! We are team Traffic Balancer AI.

Traffic congestion isn't just frustrating — traditional traffic systems fail because they treat roads in isolation. When a system fixes a jam at Junction A by diverting traffic, it secretly creates a bigger gridlock at Junction B two blocks over.

Our system, Smart City Traffic Balancer, is an autonomous congestion management platform managing 20 city zones. It evaluates multi-factor trade-offs, predicts second-order spillover traffic, and gives traffic engineers complete transparency into every decision.

I'll hand over to Person A to explain our data world."*

⏱️ PHASE 2: Data Architecture & Simulator (0:45 – 1:30)
Speaker: Person A (Data Engineer)
Screen Action: Person D points mouse to the top 4 KPI cards and clicks Simulate Tick on the sidebar.

Person A: *"Thanks Person D! As the Data Engineer, I built our underlying 20-zone urban dataset in zones_seed.json and our traffic feed engine in feed.py.

We model 20 distinct city zones categorized into Arterials, Highways, and Local roads. Each zone tracks live speeds, free-flow limits, road capacities, and event countdowns like concerts or sports matches.

[Person D clicks 'Simulate Tick']

Every click of 'Simulate Tick' advances simulation time by 5 minutes. Our feed engine combines realistic random-walk traffic drift with scripted event surges — ensuring that during live stress tests, our system detects real-time bottlenecks smart-on-cue.

Now Person B will walk you through our AI reasoning engine."*

⏱️ PHASE 3: AI Reasoning & Multi-Factor Scoring (1:30 – 2:30)
Speaker: Person B (AI Engineer)
Screen Action: Person D clicks Tab 2: Zone Analysis and selects Z05 · Silk Board Junction from the dropdown.

Person B: *"Thanks Person A! As the AI Engineer, I built the multi-factor reasoning core in agent.py.

Notice here for Silk Board Junction (Z05):

No Black Box: We reject opaque, unexplainable AI decisions.
Four Grounding Factors: Look at the first bar chart — the agent scores Congestion, Event Pressure, Diversion Impact, and Time Sensitivity.
Strategy Fit: Look at the second bar chart — the agent evaluates all 3 levers simultaneously (Signal Timing, Toll Adjustment, Route Notification). Because Z05 has high arterial congestion and event pressure, it selected Signal Timing.
Reasoning Rationale: Under 'Decision Rationale', the agent outputs a structured audit trace detailing its baseline comparisons, assumptions (like 25% driver compliance), and falsification criteria.
I'll pass to Person C to show our safety net and spillover model."*

⏱️ PHASE 4: Spillover Propagation & Human Override (2:30 – 3:20)
Speaker: Person C (Backend & Integration)
Screen Action:

Person D highlights the yellow Spillover Warning Banner on Tab 2 (Originating from Z05...).
Person D scrolls down to Operator Override, selects Signal Timing, types Officer on-site, and clicks Apply.
Person C: *"Thanks Person B! As Backend & Integration Engineer, I built the safety guardrails, second-order spillover model, and persistent state pipeline in zones_live.json.

Look at this yellow warning banner: when Z05 diverts vehicles, our propagate_spillover() algorithm calculates that neighboring Old Airport Road (Z17) absorbed +11.2% extra density. The system explicitly identifies the origin zone so downstream signals can prepare before cars arrive.

[Person D clicks 'Apply' on Override]

Furthermore, in real-world deployments, traffic officers must retain authority. When an operator forces an override, our system instantly applies the physical throughput improvements — dropping congestion from 92% to 78% — while locking out automated changes."*
